## What Is Ventoy

Ventoy is a tool that turns a USB drive into a multiboot hub for ISO files. Instead of writing one image at a time, you prepare the drive once with Ventoy, then copy as many ISO files as fit. When the computer starts from the USB, Ventoy boot shows a menu and lets you pick the image you want. This simple idea changes how people carry and test bootable media.

The name Ventoy is often searched together with Ventoy multiboot, Ventoy ISO, Ventoy USB, Ventoy boot, and Ventoy review. Each phrase points to the same practical goal: a single flash drive that holds many images and remains easy to update. Ventoy does not require you to unpack or convert each file. The ISO stays intact, and the Ventoy USB stays ready for the next task.

For people who test Linux distributions, Ventoy multiboot removes the repeated cycle of formatting and writing. For technicians who carry rescue tools, a Ventoy USB keeps several recovery images in one place. For anyone who writes a Ventoy review, the main point is usually the same: the drive is prepared once, and daily use becomes a matter of copying files and choosing from the Ventoy boot menu.

Ventoy ISO support also helps with long-term storage. You can keep older images on the same Ventoy USB and add newer ones as they arrive. When a machine needs a specific version, Ventoy boot lists what is present. This makes Ventoy a practical companion for labs, repair desks, classrooms, and personal workstations.

A Ventoy review should also mention that the tool does not force a single workflow. Some people keep a large Ventoy USB with many ISO files. Others use a small drive with two or three images. Ventoy multiboot adapts to both habits, and Ventoy boot remains the same simple menu. That consistency is why searches for Ventoy ISO, Ventoy USB, and Ventoy review often lead to the same place.
